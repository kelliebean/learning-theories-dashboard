Module #1

Behaviorism (Skinner, Pavlov, Thorndike)

Learning Objectives for Educators

  • Identify observable stimuli and reinforcement loops in instructional design.
  • Structure immediate feedback mechanisms to reinforce desired skill mastery.

Educator Overview

Behaviorism posits that learning is an observable change in behavior shaped entirely by environmental stimuli, reinforcement, and consequences. Rather than focusing on internal mental states, instructors design structured tasks where correct responses receive immediate positive reinforcement (praise, digital badges, points), extinguishing unreinforced actions over time.

Scenario Check: An online training module automatically awards learners a digital certificate and points upon completing a safety checklist correctly. Which mechanism is this?
Module #2

Cognitivism (Gagné, Neisser, Anderson)

Learning Objectives for Educators

  • Design instructional hooks that activate prior knowledge and existing schemas.
  • Organize complex content into manageable chunks to optimize working memory retrieval.

Educator Overview

Cognitivism treats the human mind like a high-powered computer. Learning is an internal mental process where incoming information is encoded, categorized, organized into existing mental schemata, and stored in long-term memory. Instructors bridge unfamiliar concepts by explicitly linking them to students' prior knowledge.

Scenario Check: Before introducing how gills extract oxygen from water, a science teacher reviews how human lungs exchange oxygen from air. What is this instructional strategy?
Module #3

Experiential Learning (John Dewey)

Learning Objectives for Educators

  • Create hands-on problem-solving tasks coupled with guided reflection.
  • Connect abstract textbook principles directly to tangible real-world experiments.

Educator Overview

John Dewey's progressive education emphasizes that genuine learning requires active, purposeful experience combined with critical reflection. Through the principles of continuity (how experiences shape future learning) and interaction, students encounter authentic problems, form hypotheses, test solutions, and reflect on outcomes.

Scenario Check: After reading about acids and bases, students mix baking soda and vinegar to observe the fizzing reaction firsthand and reflect on results. Whose framework is this?
Module #4

Cognitive Constructivism (Piaget & Inhelder)

Learning Objectives for Educators

  • Elicit student misconceptions to foster productive cognitive conflict (disequilibrium).
  • Design exploratory tasks that require schema assimilation and accommodation.

Educator Overview

Jean Piaget viewed learners as active builders of their own knowledge. When new evidence contradicts existing mental models (disequilibrium), students experience cognitive conflict and must either assimilate the data or accommodate by restructuring their mental schema to restore equilibrium.

Scenario Check: Students weigh various objects and realize small objects can be heavier than large ones, creating confusion that forces them to revise their understanding of density. What is this?
Module #5

Discovery Learning & Scaffolding (Jerome Bruner)

Learning Objectives for Educators

  • Guide learners to go "beyond the information given" to discover underlying principles.
  • Structure spiral curricula that revisit core concepts across enactive, iconic, and symbolic modes.

Educator Overview

Jerome Bruner argued that instruction should encourage learners to discover principles on their own. By translating ideas across physical actions (enactive), visual diagrams (iconic), and language/symbols (symbolic), teachers provide structured intuition and spiral back to core concepts with increasing depth.

Scenario Check: A teacher tells students that mixing primary colors produces new colors, then gives them paint to mix and discover secondary colors independently. Whose model is this?
Module #6

Sociocultural Theory (Lev Vygotsky)

Learning Objectives for Educators

  • Incorporate social dialogue and More Knowledgeable Others (MKOs) into lessons.
  • Provide targeted scaffolding within each student's Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD).

Educator Overview

Vygotsky demonstrated that cognitive development begins socially (interpsychological) before being internalized (intrapsychological), mediated by language and cultural tools. Effective instruction happens within the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) with expert scaffolding.

Scenario Check: A clay artist demonstrates hand-building, answers questions, and guides students as they shape vessels within their ZPD. Which theory is this?
Module #7

Situated Learning & Communities of Practice (Lave & Wenger)

Learning Objectives for Educators

  • Design authentic, real-world community tasks rather than isolated textbook problems.
  • Support Legitimate Peripheral Participation (LPP) where newcomers transition to full members.

Educator Overview

Situated learning views knowledge as inseparable from social practice and culture. Newcomers enter a Community of Practice through Legitimate Peripheral Participation (LPP)—performing low-risk, authentic tasks alongside experienced practitioners before graduating to full participation.

Scenario Check: Students explore the Scratch community, inspect code written by experienced creators, and perform a low-risk remix. Which framework describes this?
Module #8

Funds of Knowledge (González, Moll, & Amanti)

Learning Objectives for Educators

  • Conduct ethnographic inquiries (visitas) to uncover household and cultural labor practices.
  • Design curriculum that bridges students' lived household expertise with academic standards.

Educator Overview

Funds of knowledge recognizes that historically marginalized households possess rich, accumulated practical and cultural assets. When teachers act as ethnographic researchers, they bridge students' household strengths directly into rigorous academic instruction, countering deficit perspectives.

Scenario Check: A teacher assigns a project where students list household members, roles, and chores, using that information to connect home skills to curriculum. What is this?
